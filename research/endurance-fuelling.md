## Answer

Review fuelling when an outside endurance plan or event makes it relevant.
Timing and practical tolerance matter alongside the existing nutrition strategy.
This note supplies no default carbohydrate dose or calorie target.

## Evidence

**Claim 1.** The joint position and later athletics consensus cover varied
training and competition settings, not a universal recreational-runner
prescription. This note uses low confidence because it summarizes expert
guidance and individual application remains uncertain.

**Claim 2.** The athletics consensus distinguishes pre-event, within-event
and between-event nutrition. The distance chapter covers runners and race
walkers across different event lengths, primarily competitive contexts:
brief easy activity and a prolonged demanding event need different reviews.
Recovery timing depends partly on when another demanding bout follows.

**Claim 3.** The distance chapter balances fuel and fluid opportunities
against practical costs and gastrointestinal discomfort. Ordinary foods or
sports foods may suit different situations; this is not a supplement requirement.

**Current-source and correction check (2026-10-11).** The
[2017 erratum linked from PMID26920240](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.11.008)
removes a postgraduate-course endorsement involving Sports Oracle because
of an undisclosed potential conflict of interest; it does not amend the
carbohydrate guidance. The older source is retained only for the general
individualization principle, alongside the 2019 consensus.

Morton-2026 revisits during-exercise guidance using sport-specific studies
and practical perspectives. It highlights uncertainty around very high
intakes and the need for more realistic performance studies in both sexes.
It is a narrative review, not replacement professional consensus; oxidation
measurements and trained-athlete protocols do not set a universal intake.
No new numeric advice is adopted from it.

## Applying it

- With nutrition coaching enabled, review only missing context: expected
  duration and intensity, current meals/fuelling, practical access, gut
  tolerance and the outside plan's advice. Reuse what is already known.
- Discuss before/during/after considerations without calculating a default
  dose. Any later numeric proposal needs a directly supporting source,
  evidence population, event duration/context, confidence and athlete review;
  save athlete-specific research in a local note.
- Keep MacroFactor's calorie ownership and the existing calorie/protein
  targets. A potential conflict goes through `/goals` and its existing
  override process, never a silent extra target or automatic race plan.
- With nutrition coaching declined or deferred, leave fuelling questions out
  unless the athlete asks to revisit that choice. For medical nutrition
  concerns or an individualized clinical plan, refer to a qualified dietitian.

## Open questions

- How do the athlete's gut tolerance and food access constrain an agreed plan?
- How well does the evidence match their event, experience and environment?
- What new performance evidence changes the case for any proposed number?
