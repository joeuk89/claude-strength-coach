## Answer

Adult sleep guidance and two small dieting trials support caution about
short sleep during caloric restriction. The framework retains its
low-confidence 14-day, under-7-hour maintenance heuristic, accepting
manual hours as well as tracker evidence. This is not a medically proven
permission boundary for dieting.

## Evidence

**Claim 1.** The AASM/SRS consensus (Watson et al. 2015) recommends at
least seven hours regularly for adult health. It concerns general health,
not eligibility for a calorie deficit, and does not validate this gate.

**Claim 2.** Nedeltcheva et al. (2010) studied 10 adults with overweight
in a randomised crossover experiment: 14 days of moderate caloric
restriction with 8.5 or 5.5 hours of sleep opportunity. The shorter
opportunity reduced fat loss by 55% and increased fat-free mass loss by
60%. Time in bed is not measured sleep duration. Wang et al. (2018)
randomised 36 adults with overweight or obesity to eight weeks of caloric
restriction with or without sleep restriction on five nights weekly.
Weight loss was similar, but the proportion lost as fat was smaller in
the restricted group; catch-up sleep may not fully reverse this effect.
Neither study tested resistance-trained athletes or this exact gate.

**Claim 3.** The seven-hour line draws on adult health guidance, while
the dieting trials motivate caution. The 14-day averaging window,
maintenance response and eligibility of manual/tracker hours are coaching
choices, not experimentally validated thresholds or measurement methods.
Accepting a self-reported mean does not make it objective measurement.
There is no tested safe graded deficit algorithm here.

**Claim 4.** The no-hours fallback is precautionary coaching. A complete
dated manual diary or explicitly identified period mean can resolve
pending status; vague sleep quality and incomplete coverage cannot.
This rule does not establish that maintenance guarantees lean-mass
preservation for an individual athlete.

**Claim 5.** Tasali et al. (2022) randomised 80 adults with overweight
who usually slept under 6.5 hours. Sleep-hygiene counselling increased
sleep by about 1.2 hours and reduced energy intake by about 270 kcal/day
against control. This does not test a seven-hour dieting boundary.

## Applying it

- `/goals` and `/check-in` use `reference/nutrition-sleep.md` for the
  retained gate, no-hours fallback, manual entry, provenance and consent.
- Preserve original source dates: a period mean can inform the nutrition
  average, but cannot establish how many individual nights were short
  for the separate seven-day fatigue trigger.
- Passing the gate invites a goals review; it never automatically changes
  the phase. Maintenance recomp targets zero drift, whether sleep-gated
  or voluntary. Sustained gain and loss both need review of the observation
  and maintenance estimate; intentional continued loss requires `/goals`.
- No-app calorie changes remain the existing consent-based 100–150 kcal
  heuristic after sustained trend review; apps own their calorie changes.

## Open questions

- Whether the body-composition effects hold for lifters training hard and
  eating enough protein: the dieting trials did not test this population.
- How errors in diaries, recalled means and consumer trackers affect
  decisions near the heuristic line; no new error cutoff is assumed.
