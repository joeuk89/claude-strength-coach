## Answer

Progressive calisthenics build strength and size about as well as loaded
work of the same movement, provided the variation keeps getting harder.
Keep each variation within about 5–30 reps near failure. Move one rung at
a time, to less assistance or harder leverage, once every set hits its
target.

## Evidence

**Claim 1.** Kotarsky et al. (2018) randomised moderately trained men to
progressive push-up variations or the bench press, 3 days a week for 4
weeks: both groups raised bench 1RM similarly, and the push-up group
gained more push-up reps. Calatayud et al. (2015) matched band-loaded
6RM push-ups to the 6RM bench press by muscle activity in trained
students and found "similar gains" in 1RM over 5 weeks. Kikuchi and
Nakazato (2017) matched push-ups (adjusted, e.g. kneeling) to 40 % of
bench 1RM over 8 weeks and found "comparably effective" hypertrophy and
strength. The trials are small, short and limited to the push-up, so the
claim carries low confidence; no meta-analysis of calisthenics was found
(searched 2026-10-11).

**Claim 2.** The load meta-analyses (Schoenfeld et al. 2017; Lopez et al.
2021) show that hypertrophy is similar across loads when sets reach
failure, and that strength favours heavier loads. A bodyweight variation
is just a load, so the same rep band applies. The 30-rep cut-off is a
coaching heuristic: very light loads are less studied and far more
tiring per set, and a variation that allows more than 30 reps no longer
trains strength.

**Claim 3.** The trials above progressed by changing the variation
(Kotarsky) or the body position (Kikuchi) to keep the load matched, which
is what a ladder of assistance or leverage rungs does. The one-rung step
and its trigger mirror double progression and are a heuristic; no trial
compares step sizes.

## Applying it

- Build a ladder for each skill: for a pull-up, band or machine
  assistance from heavy to none, then added load; for a push-up, incline,
  floor, deficit, then weighted or single-arm leverage.
- Use the same RPE targets as loaded work; a bodyweight set near failure
  is a hard set and counts toward weekly dose like any other.
- When a lifter cannot do the next rung for the bottom of the rep range,
  keep the current rung and build reps.

## Open questions

- No trials of progressive calisthenics for the lower body or the pull-up
  were found.
- Whether bodyweight progressions transfer to loaded 1RM in trained
  lifters over longer than 8 weeks.
